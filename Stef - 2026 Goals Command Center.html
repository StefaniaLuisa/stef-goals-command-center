Connect Productive to fill this in

Hours, nights, weekends, and what you logged come from Productive's time entries. That needs a read-only API token, so it runs in the scheduled refresh, not in this page.

  1. In Productive, open Settings → API integrations and create a new personal API token (read access is enough).
  2. Copy the token and your Organization ID (the number in your Productive URL, likely 2650).
  3. Open productive-config.json in this folder and paste them in. Do not paste the token into chat.
  4. Say "refresh my tracker" or wait for the Monday morning auto-run.

How client calls are graded

Every client call is scored against the same nine criteria. Internal calls are not graded. Every point awarded is tied to something actually said on the call itself — the whole rubric is evidenced from the transcript, with nothing depending on a second system.

Read the scale correctly. This is not a percentage-correct scale. On current evidence a well-run client call lands roughly between 74 and 84 — provisional, drawn from only two scored calls, and it will firm up as the backlog fills in. Scoring above 90 means hitting nearly every criterion on a single call, which is rare and is not the expectation. Grades below C indicate a pattern worth addressing, not a failed meeting.

Grade scale

ScoreGradeWhat it means
95–100A+Exceptional. Genuinely rare.
88–94AStrong across every category.
84–87A−Strong call with one soft area.
80–83B+Solid. Clear single thing to tighten.
75–79BSolid. Two areas to work on.
70–74B−Fine call, several habits showing up.
60–69CA pattern worth addressing.
under 60ReviewWorth rewatching before the next one.

The three categories total 80 raw points and are normalised to a 100-point scale, so the letters below mean the same thing they always did. The scale itself is versioned as rubric v2 and is not re-tuned after the fact — a grade that can move down is the only kind worth showing.

A · Listening and client engagement — 30 points

CriterionPtsFull marks means
Open, diagnostic questions10Asked about their goal, their constraint, or why now — not just yes/no confirmations.
Handled concerns without defensiveness10Acknowledged the concern before explaining the reasoning behind it.
Floor discipline10Did not talk over anyone, and did not cut yourself off or wait to be handed the mic on your own territory.

B · Strategic value — 30 points

CriterionPtsFull marks means
Substantive recommendation10Offered a specific course of action and the reasoning behind it, not just options.
Tied back to goals, scope, or budget10Connected a request to the project plan, remaining hours, or retainer.
Named a risk or tradeoff10Raised something the client had not raised themselves.

C · Meeting control — 20 points

CriterionPtsFull marks means
Purpose stated up front5Said what the call was for and what needed deciding.
Time managed by you5You flagged the clock, not the client, and the call ended on time.
Stayed on the decisions needed10Got the decisions the meeting existed to get.

Talk-share is shown, not scored

Talk-share appears next to each grade as context, with the typical range for that kind of meeting. It carries no points, because the right number depends entirely on the meeting: a hands-on training session where you are teaching should be high, and a design review where someone else presents should be low.

Meeting typeTypical share for you
Discovery / new business20–40%
Recurring client check-in30–50%
Working session / training50–70%
Design or deliverable review20–35%

Guardrails

  • Not scored. If the transcript misattributes speakers — several people collapsed into “Speaker 1” — the call is marked Not scored rather than given a bad score.
  • Internal calls are not graded and carry no coaching feedback.
  • Trend over level. A single call’s grade means little on a sample of five to eight client calls a month. The rolling eight-call average is the honest unit.
